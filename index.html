01 / Engagement

Scope, method and what was delivered

Cognite's platform is strongest in continuous process manufacturing. The commercial question is not whether the product works — it is which companies have a problem expensive enough, and public enough, to fund a solution this quarter.

Objective
Qualify and sequence a manufacturing target list that AEs can work immediately
Method
AI-assisted discovery across earnings calls, SEC filings and leadership sources, human-verified
Output
Tiered account book, outreach architecture, handoff standard, 120-day plan
Verification
Every trigger and executive traced to a dated primary source

Step 1 — Define the ICP from the product, not the TAM

Continuous process: chemicals, refining, metals, pulp and paper, power. Multiple sites, brownfield equipment, a historian per plant, and a maintenance system that does not talk to it. Discrete assembly was deliberately excluded — the contextualization problem is weaker there and the sales cycle is longer.

Step 2 — Mine public language for stated pain

We parsed two to four quarters of earnings transcripts, 10-K risk factors, press releases and leadership announcements per company. The filter is narrow on purpose: a problem the company has said out loud, with a number or a deadline attached. Inferred pain was discarded.

Step 3 — Map the owner of the outcome

For each account, the executive accountable for the stated number, plus the operator two levels down who lives with the problem. Titles verified against company leadership pages, filings or transcripts, with a date.

Step 4 — Tier by sellability, not by size

Tier 1 requires a verified trigger, a verified named owner, and clean ICP fit. Tier 2 is strong fit with an unstable buying centre or an unverified champion. Watchlist is large but currently blocked — incumbent vendor, restructuring, or no identifiable owner.

Step 5 — Build the motion before the list is worked

Sequences, qualification criteria, the handoff record and the reporting are designed with the list, not after it. A target book without an operating system behind it becomes a spreadsheet nobody opens in week three.

What AI did, and what it did not

AI read several hundred documents and surfaced candidate signals in hours rather than weeks. Every signal was then checked against the primary source by hand. Where an executive could not be verified against a current source, this brief says so rather than guessing — a fabricated contact in a CRM is worse than a blank field.

02 / Market

What manufacturing buyers are struggling with

This list came first. Before a single account was selected we documented the twelve problems that recur underneath almost every manufacturing conversation — then searched for companies that had stated one of them publicly, with a number attached. Each card shows what a prospect says, what is actually happening underneath, and which qualified accounts it points to.

Why pain-first beats company-first

Anyone can pull a list of large manufacturers. What makes a list worth an AE's calendar is knowing, before the first dial, which specific problem you are calling about and why that company has it this quarter. Working pain-first is what converts a company name into a reason to call.

The qualifying signal we weight highest

A public cost or reliability commitment. Process manufacturers are in a margin downturn: capex capped at maintenance levels, headcount down, and boards promised EBITDA improvement that must now come out of the existing asset base. That is a funded, board-visible, time-boxed problem — the best kind to sell into.

How this shortens the cycle

Most industrial deals stall because the first meeting spends forty minutes rediscovering the problem. When the seller arrives with pain mapped to a named owner mapped to a funded commitment, the conversation opens at the business case instead of at discovery. Fewer meetings to the same decision.

A. Segment the territory by how the deal actually gets bought

Cluster 1 — Arizona / Southwest

In-person accounts, run from Tempe

Freeport-McMoRan (Phoenix), Salt River Project (Tempe), Arizona Public Service (Phoenix), Intel Ocotillo (Chandler). All within driving distance of Cognite's US headquarters.

  • Field-led motion: site visits, lunch meetings, an AE who can be in the building the same afternoon a meeting is booked.
  • SRP is the fastest path of the four — board-governed, no state rate regulator in the procurement chain, and a Senior Director of OT Service already on record about IT/OT convergence.
  • Use local presence as the differentiator against remote competitors. This cluster should carry a higher meeting-to-opportunity conversion rate than the others; if it does not, the problem is execution, not the market.
Cluster 2 — Gulf Coast chemicals

Highest dollar-denominated pain, densest buying centre

Westlake, LyondellBasell, Dow. Houston-anchored, continuous process, and all three have publicly committed to EBITDA improvement from the existing asset base.

  • Reliability is the wedge. One LyondellBasell outage cost roughly $250M of EBITDA; Westlake's CEO named reliability as the one pillar of a $600M plan that is behind.
  • Expect long, committee-driven cycles. Multi-thread from day one — segment president downward and reliability manager upward.
  • Competitive caution: Dow's Univation subsidiary partnered with C3 AI on predictive maintenance already deployed across 50+ steam cracker furnaces. Do not lead with APM at Dow. Lead with the contextualized data layer and non-furnace asset classes.
Cluster 3 — National process & materials

Event-driven, longer horizon, larger landing zones

Alcoa, Eastman Chemical, International Paper. Each has a structural event that creates a data problem with a deadline attached.

  • Alcoa's $4.1B South32 acquisition carries a ~$900M synergy target closing H1 2027 — acquired-asset data harmonization with a board-visible number on it.
  • Eastman's own 2026 earnings bridge includes “improved manufacturing utilization including fewer shutdowns,” and their Microsoft Fabric programme covers lab data with no OT side. That gap is the opening.
  • International Paper's split into two public companies means two data estates must be stood up. Best-timed wedge on the list and the least stable buying centre — open at the operating P&L, not at IT.

B. The motion, start to handoff

Plays

Four repeatable plays, selected by trigger type

  • The stated-commitment play. A public EBITDA or reliability target that is behind. Open by quoting their own call. Highest conversion, use wherever available.
  • The integration play. An acquisition or divestiture with a synergy number and a close date. Sell cross-site data harmonization before the integration team picks someone else.
  • The stalled-pilot play. A company that ran an AI pilot that never reached plant two. Sell the foundation, not the model.
  • The new-executive play. A CIO, CDO or operations leader inside their first year. Sell the mandate they were hired to deliver.
Multi-threading

Two entry points per account, always

Top-down to the executive who owns the committed number, bottom-up to the reliability or digital manager who lives with the problem. The manager answers more often and knows more; the executive controls the budget. Running only one of the two is the most common reason an industrial deal stalls at month four.

Proof

Lead with the line that survives scrutiny

NOVA Chemicals publicly described operational data retrieval going from hours to about a minute. That claim is attributable, human and instantly believable to anyone who has worked a plant. Vendor-modelled ROI figures are used only as directional support and always labelled as commissioned — a number a seller cannot defend in discovery is worse than no number.

Qualification

Four criteria before anything moves to an AE

  • A specific operational problem stated in the prospect's own words.
  • The executive who owns the outcome, identified by name.
  • A reason it matters this quarter — a committed number, a deadline, an event.
  • A scheduled and accepted next meeting with the AE on it.

C. The 120-day execution plan

Days 1–30 — Foundation

Instrument before scaling

  • Load the ten accounts and verified stakeholders into the CRM with trigger, source and date on every record.
  • Agree the written definition of a qualified opportunity with sales leadership, including worked examples of accepted and rejected handoffs.
  • Stand up the dashboard: touches by channel, reply rate, meetings booked, handoffs accepted, opportunities created. Define every number before anyone is measured on it.
  • Launch cluster 1 only. Small enough to learn from, local enough to correct fast.
Days 31–60 — Proof

Find the play that converts

  • Run all four plays across clusters 1 and 2; measure reply and meeting rates by play, not in aggregate.
  • First AE handoffs delivered under the written standard. Every one marked accepted, recycled or rejected with one line of why.
  • Kill the two weakest sequences. Rewrite from the language of prospects who actually replied.
  • Target: first qualified opportunities sourced and accepted.
Days 61–90 — Scale

Widen the list, hold the bar

  • Expand the account book from 10 to 40 using the same qualification method — Olin, Chemours, Huntsman, Nucor, Cleveland-Cliffs, Georgia-Pacific, Linde, Air Products, Marathon Petroleum, Phillips 66.
  • Open cluster 3 with the integration and stalled-pilot plays.
  • Add an events motion: pre-event outreach to a named target list, meetings booked before arrival, follow-up out within 24 hours.
  • Publish weekly pipeline forecast to leadership from the dashboard, not from memory.
Days 91–120 — Systematize

Make it survive the person who built it

  • Document the playbook: triggers, plays, sequences, qualification criteria, handoff template, dashboard definitions.
  • Quarterly trigger refresh built into the calendar — earnings season becomes a standing research sprint, not an ad hoc scramble.
  • Review conversion by play, by cluster and by title to decide where quarter two's effort goes.
  • Hand the system to the next rep and verify they can run it without the author in the room.

D. What gets measured

Leading

Touches by channel. Accounts multi-threaded (target: 100% with two or more contacts engaged). Reply rate by play. Meetings booked.

Qualifying

Handoffs delivered, and the accept / recycle / reject split with reasons. This is the single most important number in the system — it measures trust, not activity.

Pipeline

Qualified opportunities created, sourced pipeline value, stage-two conversion, and time from first touch to first AE meeting.

Learning

Which play converted, which cluster outperformed, which titles answered. Reviewed weekly and acted on, not archived.

Email 1 — Brian J. Powers, SVP PEM, Westlake

Subject: the pillar that's behind

Brian —

On the August 11 call the three pillars got split two and one: footprint and cost ahead of plan, plant reliability “not yet where we wanted to be.” And capex is held at depreciation, so the fix has to come out of the base you already own.

In most chlor-alkali and PVC chains we look at, the constraint isn't instrumentation. It's that the data that would have flagged the upset eight hours earlier sits in the historian, the maintenance system and the P&IDs, and nobody can see it in one place. NOVA Chemicals went from hours to about a minute on exactly that.

Worth twenty minutes with someone who has done this in a PVC chain?

Voicemail — under 20 seconds

“Brian, calling about the reliability pillar from your August call — the one that's behind while the other two are ahead.

We work with NOVA Chemicals and Celanese on precisely that problem. I've got one specific idea about your PVC chain and I'll be brief. [number]. Thanks.”

Named trigger, named peers, a specific idea, a time commitment. No product description — that is what the call is for.

LinkedIn — day 11

“Brian — I've sent a couple of notes about the reliability pillar of the $600M plan, so I'll stop after this one.

The thing I'd want to know in your seat: when a unit trips, how long does it take to reconstruct what happened across the historian, the work orders and the drawings? If the answer is days, that's the number we move.

Happy to be told it's not a priority.”

Break-up — day 14

Subject: wrong person?

Brian —

I may have aimed this at the wrong desk. If reliability across the PEM sites sits with someone else, I'd rather talk to them than keep landing in your inbox.

Who should I be speaking with?

A referral is a better outcome than a reluctant meeting, and this email gets answered more than the four before it.

07 / Standards

How Wide Open Sky AI works

The methodology is only worth something if it holds up under pressure. These are the operating rules behind every engagement.

A name is not an opportunity

We do not deliver contacts who took a call. We deliver a confirmed pain, a named owner of that pain, a plausible funding path, and a next meeting already on the calendar.

Verified or flagged, never guessed

Every executive is traced to a dated primary source. Where that fails, the record says so. A fabricated contact in a CRM costs more than an empty field.

Vendor numbers are labelled as vendor numbers

Commissioned ROI studies are directional and said to be. The claims we put in a seller's mouth are the ones they can defend when a buyer asks who audited them.

AI reads, humans decide

AI processes hundreds of documents in hours. It is not reliable at judging whether a signal is commercially real, and it states stale facts with the same confidence as current ones. The judgment layer is the work.

Rejected handoffs are the best data

Every handoff is marked accepted, recycled or rejected with one line of why, and reviewed weekly. That loop is how a pipeline engine improves instead of just getting busier.

Built to be handed over

Deliverables include the playbook, the definitions and the dashboard logic — so the system survives the person who built it.
